# H6: the ordering rule of ten validators in November 2026

Status: frozen once the SHA-256 of this exact file is on chain. It goes into a Memo before the first probe of the block
(`python -m tiepairs --block P prereg --live`), together with the hashes of the code and `targets-P.json`, and the same hash
seeds every coin of the block. Changing any of the three after that makes `run --live` refuse to start.

## Question

For each validator: what decides which of our transactions lands earlier, how strictly that holds, and whether there is a
rule at all. Measured on the versions running in November, because most of the published targets have changed version or
client since the published measurements of 24–26 October.

## Why these predictions, before any data

- The published studies identify reward ÷ (cost + 1) on six core targets, among CU price, lamport fee, cost alone, arrival
  and no rule. Reward is the priority fee plus the unburned half of the signature fee; cost is the requested limits.
- The priority formula did not change between Agave v4.3.2 and v4.4.0, read at both tags. Firedancer v26.10.5 computes the same
  reward in `fd_pack.c`, half of 5,000 lamports per signature plus the priority fee, and compares reward ÷ cost without the +1.
  No probe here sits close enough for that +1 to matter.
- So the prediction for every validator is reward ÷ (cost + 1) again, with half the signature fee counted, and the block
  exists to catch a validator where a new version, a client change or a builder has moved it. BAM, Harmonic and Rakurai are
  where a surprise would come from: we could not read how they sort.
- Ties are a separate matter. Agave #14836 orders equal priority by arrival from 4.4.0 on (`H5.md`). Published data put
  Firedancer the other way round, later-sent first in 50 of 54 pairs on 26.10.4.
- Inside the window the published overtaking counts fit batches of a fixed period (`design/horizon_fit.py`): 2.5 ms on Agave and
  Jito-Solana, 15.5 ms on `JUPiTERr`, 28 ms on `Fd7btgyS`, 50 ms on `DRpbCBMx`, and at least 221 ms on Firedancer `EvnRmnMr`.
- This file was frozen after block T ended, with its verdict known: H5 supported, the first-sent probe first in 90 of 90 pairs
  on three JitoLabs 4.4.0 validators against 46 of 90 on three JitoLabs 4.3.2 controls.

## Design

- **Targets** (`targets-P.json`, checked against a fresh `getClusterNodes` before the plan and again before each window against
  the latest snapshot, refreshed about once a minute):

  | Validator | Client | Version | Ingress |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `7QQGNm3p` | Agave | 4.3.2 | direct |
  | `HEL1USMZ` | JitoLabs | 4.4.0 | direct |
  | `3SkE34PV` | JitoLabs | 4.3.2 | direct |
  | `9eGrDohd` | Firedancer | 26.10.5 | direct |
  | `ana2y2Yv` | Rakurai | 4.3.2 | direct |
  | `DRpbCBMx` | AgaveBam | 4.4.0 | shared |
  | `5Cchr1XG` | AgaveBam | 4.3.2 | shared |
  | `Fd7btgyS` | HarmonicAgave | 4.4.0 | shared |
  | `JUPiTERr` | HarmonicFiredancer | 26.10.5 | shared |
  | `9jxgosAf` | FireBAM | 26.10.5 | shared |

  Eight client names and ten client and version pairs. Stake per target 0.4–3.9%. Ingress is reported and does not change the
  design: every target gets the same kinds, in the same numbers, at the same moments, so a difference between clients is not a
  difference of design. A target that shows another client or version stops there, keeps its pairs and is reported as moved.
- **Probe:** the published shape. Legacy, one signature, two write locks (payer and one shared lock account per pair), a
  zero-lamport transfer and three compute-budget instructions, no Memo, 600 CU executed. S-two adds a second signer, read-only
  and without an account: a throwaway key, new on every start of the program, that holds nothing and shows in the signed bytes.
  That is 5,000 more lamports, 720 more cost units and no further write lock.

  | Probe | CU limit | CU price, µlamports | Loaded data limit | Fee, lamports | Cost | Reward | Priority |
  |---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | A | 1,000 | 1,000,000 | 32 KiB | 6,000 | 2,335 | 3,500 | 1,498,287 |
  | Y | 1,500 | 4,000,000 | 32 KiB | 11,000 | 2,835 | 8,500 | 2,997,179 |
  | D | 1,000 | 3,000,000 | 64 MiB | 8,000 | 18,711 | 5,500 | 293,929 |
  | K-x | 1,000 | 6,000,000 | 32 KiB | 11,000 | 2,335 | 8,500 | 3,638,698 |
  | K-y | 2,000 | 4,250,000 | 32 KiB | 13,500 | 3,335 | 11,000 | 3,297,362 |
  | VH, v1 | 1,000 | fee 1,000 | 32 KiB | 6,000 | 2,331 | 3,500 | 1,500,857 |
  | VL, v1 | 1,010 | fee 1,000 | 32 KiB | 6,000 | 2,341 | 3,500 | 1,494,449 |
  | S-two | 1,000 | 1,000 | 32 KiB | 10,001 | 3,055 | 5,001 | 1,636,452 |
  | S-one | 1,000 | 2,000,000 | 32 KiB | 7,000 | 2,335 | 4,500 | 1,926,369 |

  A v1 transaction has no compute-budget instructions: limits and an absolute priority fee sit in its header (SIMD-0385), so
  its cost has 3 CU of instruction data against the 7 of a legacy probe.

- **Contrasts, x against y:**

  | Name | x, y | Reward ÷ cost | CU price | Lamport fee | Cost alone | Arrival, no rule |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | C4 | Y, A | x | x | x | y | tie |
  | C6 | D, A | y | x | x | y | tie |

  Among these keys the patterns (x, y), (x, x) and (y, y) cannot be confused, and arrival or no rule gives a share near one
  half on both, with send order deciding under arrival. C4 is the published contrast. C6 is new: D pays more than A on CU
  price and on lamports and still has less reward per unit of cost, so it sets the ratio against both alternatives at once.
  The ratio prefers A for any share of the signature fee counted as reward. A ratio whose cost leaves out the loaded data
  limit would also put D first, so (x, x) is named for all three.

  **E** is two identical A probes, for the tie-break. **K** is K-x against K-y: the ratio puts K-x first whatever share of the
  signature fee it counts, an additive key reward − λ·cost puts K-y first for every λ below 2.5, a question the published work
  left open. **VH** and **VL** set a v1 probe against the legacy A: all three pay the same lamports, VH sits 0.17% above A in
  priority and VL 0.26% below. If a client gives v1 the same key, A loses to VH and beats VL whatever the send order, in the
  buffered regime. Together they pin the cost a client assigns to v1 to between 5 units below and 3 above the formula's 2,331.
  v1 was a quarter of the transactions in a block read on 1 November, and no published work sent it.

  **S1** is S-two against A and **S2** is S-two against S-one. They ask how much of the signature fee a sort counts as reward,
  which the published work left open and which C4 and C6 cannot see, because all their probes carry one signature. Under
  reward ÷ cost with a share s of the fee counted, S-two beats A once s passes 0.38 and beats S-one once s passes 0.76. So
  (x, y) brackets the half both sources use, (y, y) a smaller share and (x, x) a larger one. S-two pays one lamport of priority
  fee and not zero, so that a filter on zero-fee transactions cannot take it out.

  **W2**, **W10** and **W40** are C4 sent inside the window with Y always second, by 2, 10 and 40 ms. The reading is overtaking:
  how often Y lands first although A left earlier.
- **Regime:** everything but the W kinds is buffered: fired when slot F−5 is first seen, about 1 s before the first shred of F, so both
  probes wait until the slot starts. This holds for shared ingress too. Inside the window a 10 ms gap would hand the rule's
  loser a head start in half the pairs, and the published overtaking at 10 ms is 4/12 on `JUPiTERr` and 7/12 on `Fd7btgyS`, so
  the contrast would read the head start and not the key. Published pre-window sends to BAM and Harmonic landed in the target
  window in 28 of 29 cases. A pair that lands with another leader ends as `outside_window`. The W kinds are fired when slot F is
  first seen and cancelled if F+2 has already been seen.
- **Pair:** one per window, 10 ms apart, or at the gap of its W kind, on one QUIC connection opened about 7 s before the pair and
  closed after it. Only the first window of a leader run. At least 1,200 slots between windows of one target and 40 slots between
  any two pairs, so pairs do not share a moment and do not overlap on the payers. Every pair has its own lock account.
- **Coins:** which payer goes first is the parity of the first byte of `sha256(seed:identity:first_slot)`, which payer carries
  x is the parity of the second byte, except in the W kinds, where x goes with the payer that goes second. The kinds of a session are dealt to a target in the order of
  `sha256(seed:session:identity:kind:i)`. The seed is this file's SHA-256. Anyone can recompute all three.
- **Planned size:** three sessions at different hours, within 48 hours of the first, which opens with one pair per target as
  a smoke test. Each session gives each target C4 8, C6 8, E 8, W2 4, W10 4, W40 4, K 2, VH 2, VL 2, S1 3, S2 3, which is 48 pairs.
  That is 1,440 pairs and about 0.0223 SOL in fees, under a 0.025 SOL cap for this block and the 0.05 SOL cap of the whole
  measurement. No data-dependent stopping and no pair beyond the plan. Resuming only continues a session after its smoke test,
  after a fault, or after a pause planned to keep sending out of 01:50–02:10 UTC, when the sender's router went dark during
  block T. A session runs about 7 hours, set by the validator with the fewest windows.

## Estimand and decision

A pair is scored when both probes landed without error in the target's window, in the leader schedule's sense. "Lands
before" is the order of (slot, index in `getBlock`), as in the published studies. L(C) = P(x before y) over scored pairs.

- **Rule.** For C4 and C6 on each validator: exact two-sided binomial test against one half, Holm over all 20 tests at 0.05.

  | C4 | C6 | Named |
  |---|---|---|
  | Y first | A first | reward per cost |
  | Y first | D first | CU price, lamport fee, or a ratio without the loaded data cost |
  | A first | A first | cost alone |
  | no difference | no difference | see below |
  | one of them decided, the other no difference | | partial |
  | A first | D first | mixed |

  With no difference on both, the share of C4 and C6 pairs won by the first-sent member is tested against one half, exact
  two-sided, Holm over 10 validators: it names arrival or reverse arrival. Otherwise **no rule seen** if both 95% intervals
  sit inside (0.20, 0.80), and **undetermined** if they do not: too few pairs to tell a null from a weak rule.
- **Strict** if named reward per cost and the winner's share is at least 0.80 on both contrasts.
- **Not evaluable** with fewer than 10 scored pairs on C4 or C6 of that validator.
- **Withdrawn** for a validator whose median achieved gap on buffered pairs is more than 1 ms from 10 ms.
- **Tie-break.** P(first-sent lands first) on E, exact two-sided binomial test, Holm over the 10 validators: arrival, reverse
  arrival or no relation seen, with the same minimum of 10 pairs. Prediction: arrival on `HEL1USMZ`, reverse arrival on
  `9eGrDohd`. No prediction elsewhere. These pairs are a second reading of block T on other probes and other days.
- **K.** Pooled over the validators named reward per cost, exact two-sided binomial test, with all ten as a second line. Cost
  alone and CU price also put K-x first, lamport fee puts K-y first and arrival gives one half, so only under that rule does K
  speak about an additive key. Prediction: K-x first in at least 0.80 of scored pairs.
- **S.** Nine planned pairs per contrast per validator sit below the minimum of 10, so S is pooled, into two groups fixed here:
  the four validators whose scheduler source we read (Agave, JitoLabs, Firedancer) and the six whose sort we could not read
  (AgaveBam, HarmonicAgave, HarmonicFiredancer, FireBAM, Rakurai). Only validators named reward per cost count, because under
  another key the same pattern means something else: cost alone and CU price also give (y, y), lamport fee also gives (x, x). For
  each group and contrast, exact two-sided binomial test, Holm over the 4 tests at 0.05, with at least 10 scored pairs per cell.

  | S1 | S2 | Named |
  |---|---|---|
  | S-two first | S-two first | a share above 0.76 |
  | S-two first | S-one first | half, a share between 0.38 and 0.76 |
  | A first | S-one first | a share below 0.38 |
  | anything else | | undetermined |

  Prediction: half in both groups, with the winner's share at least 0.80 on both contrasts.

Every report gives k/n and the exact 95% Clopper–Pearson interval per validator and per session: `summary.md` shows C4, C6 and E
by session, `summary.json` every kind.

## Exploratory, no decision attached

- **In-slot arrivals.** W2, W10 and W40 per validator, 12 planned pairs at each gap: how often Y landed first although A went
  ahead. The published cells at 10 ms are 0/12 on Agave, 0/9 on Jito-Solana, 4/12 on `JUPiTERr`, 7/12 on `Fd7btgyS`, 5/5 on
  `DRpbCBMx` and 10/10 on Firedancer `EvnRmnMr`, and at 2 ms 7/35, 6/29, 32/33, 30/31, 14/14 and 31/31. For a batch of period B with
  a random phase the share is max(0, 1 − gap/B). `tools/horizon.py` fits B per validator by maximum likelihood on achieved gaps,
  with a noise floor of 0.01 and the 95% profile range, and we compare it with the published fit above. Twelve pairs place a validator on that scale, they do not
  measure B finely. At 2 ms the network alone could swap a pair, so the report gives the empirical swap bound at 2 ms beside
  the one at 10 ms.
- **Signature share per validator.** S1 and S2 shares per validator, nine planned pairs each, reported without a test.
- **v1.** VH and VL pooled by code base, Agave or Firedancer. Prediction: x first on VH and y first on VL, each in at least 0.80
  of scored pairs. With six planned pairs per validator this reads a code base, not a validator.
- **Between sessions.** The shares per session show whether a validator moved during the block. Published work saw Harmonic
  change within days on one version.
- **Between versions.** `DRpbCBMx` against `5Cchr1XG` and `HEL1USMZ` against `3SkE34PV` differ in version. This compares two
  validators, not one before and after.
- **Direct against shared ingress.** Same design on both, so a difference is a finding about the path, with operators and load
  in it too.

## Exclusions

Nothing is dropped silently. Each pair ends in one class, and the table of classes per validator is part of the report:
`not_sent` (with its reason), `none_landed`, `one_landed`, `failed`, `outside_window`, `different_slots`, `partial`,
`send_error`, `crash_unknown`, `same_slot`. `same_slot` and `different_slots` are scored.

## Sensitivity analyses

1. **Same slot:** only pairs that landed in one slot.
2. **Selection bound:** `one_landed` pairs counted against the prediction.
3. **Send order:** among scored C4 and C6 pairs the share with x sent first must be compatible with one half (p ≥ 0.01),
   otherwise the validator is flagged. The share where the first-sent member won is reported beside it.
4. **Lead:** only pairs whose later member was sent at least 150 ms, plus half the RTT, before the first shred of F as an RPC
   node stamped it.
5. **Gap:** only pairs with an achieved gap within 1 ms of 10 ms.
6. **Settled connection:** only pairs sent on a connection at least 5 s old.

## After the freeze

A bug found in the frozen code is not fixed in place, because that would break the hash. The report names it, gives the frozen
result as it came out and shows any corrected analysis beside it, from a separate tool. A session cut short is reported as cut
short, and its unsent pairs stay unsent.

## What would change our mind

- A validator named a different key: its client or version has moved the rule, and the report says so. `partial` and
  `undetermined` say only that this block could not tell, and `mixed` is a conflict no candidate key gives.
- Arrival or reverse arrival named: for buffered pairs on this path, send order beats every priced key.
- No rule seen, with both intervals inside (0.20, 0.80): on this path our probes are not ordered by any candidate key.
- K-y first: the published ratio reading is wrong for these amounts.
- S names anything but half: that group's sort does not count what a validator keeps, and its reward arithmetic differs from
  the sources we read.
- B well away from the published fit on a validator: its batching moved, or our probes reach it on another path.

## What this cannot show

- **A builder's sort against the scheduler behind it.** BAM and Harmonic are reported as the ingress path measured.
- **Arrival at the scheduler** is not observed. The send gap is, and the run samples RTT around each pair.
- **Other transactions.** Our probes are small, untipped, one or two signatures, unstaked sender. Heavy transactions, bundles and
  contended accounts are outside this block.
- **Other keys.** K excludes an additive key only for λ below 2.5, and the published work excludes λ above 10. Between them
  it stays open.
- **The exact signature share.** S gives a bracket and not a value, and it assumes the key's cost holds the 720 units of a
  signature. A sort that left them out and counted half would read as a share above 0.76. Precompile signatures, which also
  pay, are not sent.
- **The cause of a batch period.** B describes how the curve falls, and a fit to three gaps cannot tell a batch from another
  shape with the same three points.
- **Overtaking against a swap.** Y always goes second, so the curve has no in-window control. A swap inside a validator's stream
  handling reads as overtaking, and the swap bound covers the network alone.
- **The cause of a difference** between two validators on different versions: operators, load and settings differ too.
- `clientId` and version are self-reported. Observed behaviour is the only confirmation of what a validator runs.
- A verdict covers three sessions. It assumes a validator's key did not change between them, and the per-session table is
  there to show if it did.

## Operating characteristics

`python -m tiepairs power` simulates the rule with 24 planned pairs per contrast and Holm over 20 tests. With both probes of
a pair landing in 80% of pairs, a validator that follows the rule in 95% of pairs is named in 99.5% of simulated validators,
and a run names all ten in 95.7%. At 60% landing that is 85.5% and 37.1%. With no rule at all, no validator is named. Below
about 40% landing a validator tends to end as not evaluable, which the report states as such. For S, Holm over four tests with
36 and 54 planned pairs per contrast: a sort that follows half in 95% of pairs is named in 86.1% and 99.8% of simulated runs at
40% landing, and in every run at 80%. With no rule at all it is never named.
