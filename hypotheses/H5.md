# H5: equal-priority order after Agave #14836

Status: frozen once the SHA-256 of this exact file is on chain. It goes into a Memo before the first probe
(`python -m tiepairs prereg --live`), together with the hashes of the code and `targets.json`, and the same hash seeds the
send order of every pair. Changing any of the three after that makes `run --live` refuse to start.

## Claim

On builds that contain [anza-xyz/agave#14836](https://github.com/anza-xyz/agave/pull/14836), two equal-priority
transactions waiting in the leader's buffer are processed in arrival order. On builds without it their order follows a
reused slab index and is not arrival order.

## Why this prediction, before any data

- The pull request, merged 2026-08-31, replaces the tie-break of `TransactionPriorityId`. Before: equal priority falls to
  `TransactionId`, a `Slab` key that gets reused. After: equal priority falls to `arrival_order`, oldest first, a counter
  assigned once per transaction in `insert_map_only`. Its test is named
  `test_equal_priority_transactions_are_fifo_across_slab_reuse`.
- Checked by reading the file at each tag: the change is in Agave v4.4.0-beta.1, v4.4.0-rc.1, v4.4.0, and in jito-solana
  v4.4.0-rc.1-jito and v4.4.0-jito. It is not in Agave v4.3.2, the last 4.3 tag, or in
  jito-solana v4.3.2-jito and v4.3.2-jito.0.
- The priority formula itself did not change between v4.3.2 and v4.4.0.
- The same probe, measured on 4.3.2 by [eternally-black](https://github.com/eternally-black/alpenglow-validator-ordering)
  (24–25 October 2026, buffered at −150 ms, 10 ms apart, direct ingress), lands first-sent first in:
  Agave `7QQGNm3p` 7/7 and 23/35, Jito-Solana `HEL1USMZ` 5/7 and 10/29. Pooled 45/78 = 0.58; Jito alone 15/36 = 0.42. That
  report records it as "not plain FIFO", descriptive and outside any hypothesis.

## Design

- **Groups** (`targets.json`, checked against a fresh `getClusterNodes` before the plan, then again from a snapshot at
  most a minute old before each window; the client and version seen at both moments are logged per probe):
  - treated: JitoLabs 4.4.0 `HEL1USMZ`, `E1r4Psq8`, `bitHUmbK`;
  - control: JitoLabs 4.3.2 `3SkE34PV`, `8gDfqHXZ`, `privaEdS`;
  - relayer, exploratory and outside the decision: JitoLabs 4.3.2 `EvnRmnMr`, `5pPRHnie`, `GnC339vk`.

  Same client, same week, same instrument, so the contrast needs no other study's numbers. The groups were chosen before
  any probe, on two things the version does not explain. Both take transactions directly, and in a passive read of blocks
  from epochs 1067–1073 both show a priority-sorted start of the first slot and none in the third: mean head-of-block rank
  correlation 0.44–0.57 and −0.01 to 0.11. The match is partial. Treated validators hold 3.9%, 2.4% and 0.3% of stake,
  controls 0.6%, 0.4% and 0.3%, so their traffic differs. Candidates that looked like one operator's fleet were dropped from
  the control group so that it does not rest on one setup. The largest JitoLabs 4.3.2 validators are left out of the contrast
  because they advertise port 11228, the default of Jito Relayer, and their first slot looks different (−0.18 to 0.22).
  Against them the contrast would compare paths, not versions. They stay as a third arm, reported apart, with no decision
  attached. `HEL1USMZ` was on 4.3.2 in the published baseline, so it also gives a same-validator before and after.
  `hypotheses/selection/selection_report.py` recomputes these figures from the block reads behind this choice: `panel.jsonl` a008a11d9e2c855bcc3d7536da5776a109c61754831eca0d06f0d453755e35b1,
  `scan.jsonl` c572e56f6323bed3e49f34d92c06146f872ef9d97975dad9ac0267843927f4ce, `ctrl.jsonl` ddce5d0b896d96b9c33f615b4f83ffd6389d51a7951df9d0c4c0df957f333762 (SHA-256, before compression).
- **Unit:** one leader window, one pair. At least 1,200 slots, about 4 minutes, between windows of one target, so that a
  large validator's pairs span the same hours as a small one's. Only the first window of a leader run, so that another
  leader precedes it.
- **Pair:** two legacy transactions from two payers, identical in shape: one signature, two write locks (payer and one
  shared lock account, which forces the scheduler to order them), CU limit 6,000, CU price 1,420,000 µlamports, loaded
  data limit 128 KiB, a zero-lamport transfer to the lock account and an 8-byte Memo label. Priority 1,496,875, within
  0.1% of the baseline probe's 1,498,287.
- **Timing:** both streams on one QUIC connection, 10 ms apart, fired when slot F−5 is first seen, where F is the first
  slot of the window. That is about 1 s before the first shred of F, so both are waiting in the buffer when F starts. A pair
  is cancelled if slot F−3 has already been seen. The published baseline sent at −150 ms; only the waiting matters here,
  and the earlier send keeps a margin against a late slot notification. Each window gets its own connection, opened when
  slot F−40 is seen, about 7 s before the pair, and closed after it. In a dry run some servers kept an idle connection for
  minutes and others dropped it within seconds, so a link kept between windows would have given the groups connections of
  different age. If a server closes the connection before the anchor, a new one opens at once, at most 5 a minute per address.
- **Our own transactions:** every pair has its own lock account, so only its two members contend on it. All pairs share the
  two payers, so at least 40 slots, about 8 s, separate any two pairs of a run: one pair is over before the next is sent.
- **Send order:** which payer goes first is the parity of the first byte of `sha256(seed:identity:first_slot)`, with this
  file's SHA-256 as the seed. Anyone can recompute it.
- **Before the first probe:** the probe shape passes `simulateTransaction` and `getFeeForMessage` on mainnet, unsigned, and
  any difference from the plan stops the run. Live commands refuse anything but the mainnet genesis hash at slot 459,653,008
  or later.
- **Planned size:** 30 pairs per target, 90 per group, 540 transactions with the relayer arm, 0.0073 SOL in fees. The smaller
  validators have 13–23 windows an hour, so with the spacing above a run takes about 5 hours. Hard cap 0.01 SOL for this block inside a 0.05 SOL
  cap for the whole measurement. No data-dependent stopping: a target stops when its pairs run out, when it stops matching
  its expected client and version, or when the cap is reached.
- **One run decides.** The run opens with one pair per target as a smoke test. Those pairs stay in the data, and the run
  continues to 30 pairs per target whatever order they show, unless nothing lands at all. No pair is added beyond the planned
  30. Continuing after the smoke test uses the resume path of the same run, and apart from that resuming only continues a run
  stopped by a fault. A repeat on a later day is a separate block with its own Memo and
  its own cap: it is reported beside this verdict and does not change it.

## Estimand and decision

E(group) = P(first-sent member lands first), over pairs where both members landed without error in the same slot of the
target's window. "Lands first" is the smaller index in `getBlock`. The leader of a slot comes from the leader schedule,
not from the recipient of the block's Fee reward, which differs on some validators.

- **Primary test:** one-sided Fisher exact test, treated > control, on pairs pooled per group.
- **Supported** if all of these hold: p < 0.01; the treated share is at least 0.80; it exceeds the control share by at least
  0.20; and every treated validator shows at least 0.80 on at least 10 informative pairs. The share conditions are there
  because the code predicts FIFO on each treated build, not merely a shift in the pool.
- **Not supported** otherwise. This is not evidence for the opposite.
- **Not evaluable** with fewer than 20 informative pairs in the treated or control group, or fewer than 10 on a treated
  validator.
- **Withdrawn** if the median achieved send gap of the treated or control group is more than 1 ms from 10 ms.

Every report gives k/n, the share and the exact two-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson interval, per group and per validator.

## Exclusions

Nothing is dropped silently. Each pair ends in one class, and the table of classes per group is part of the report:
`not_sent` (with its reason), `none_landed`, `one_landed`, `failed`, `outside_window`, `different_slots`, `partial`,
`send_error`, `crash_unknown`, `same_slot`. Only `same_slot` enters the primary test. Every reason is also its own field
in the outcome files.

## Sensitivity analyses

1. **Window-wide:** `different_slots` pairs scored by (slot, index), which is how the 4.3.2 baseline was counted.
2. **Lead:** only pairs whose later member was sent at least 150 ms, plus half the RTT, before the first shred of F as an
   RPC node stamped it.
3. **Gap:** only pairs with an achieved gap within 1 ms of 10 ms.
4. **Selection bound:** `one_landed` pairs counted against the claim in both groups.
5. **Per validator:** if treated validators disagree, the report says so next to the pooled result.
6. **Settled connection:** only pairs sent on a connection at least 5 s old. Servers differ in how soon they close unstaked
   connections, so one group's pairs could still more often travel on a connection opened just before sending. This view
   removes that difference, and the report gives each group's share of pairs sent on a connection younger than 5 s.

## What would change our mind

- Treated and control indistinguishable: the code change does not decide the order we can see on mainnet.
- Treated clearly above control but below 0.80: something else also orders these pairs, and the FIFO reading fails.
- Control also near 1: the slab order was not what separated these pairs, and the 4.3.2 baseline does not carry over to
  this group.

## What this cannot show

- Arrival at the scheduler is not observed. The send gap is. The code orders by admission to the scheduler's container
  (`insert_map_only`), and packets reach it through parallel stages such as signature verification, so reading the result
  as arrival order assumes a 10 ms gap survives them. For the network part, both probes share one QUIC connection and the
  run samples RTT on it every 10 ms right after each pair. The report gives the share of neighbouring samples where the
  earlier one came back at least 10 ms slower, with an exact upper bound.
- `clientId` and version are self-reported. Observed behaviour is the only confirmation of what a validator runs.
- It covers JitoLabs builds with the stock scheduler. AgaveBam and Harmonic builds replace the scheduler, and Firedancer
  has its own, so nothing here speaks for them.
- Lead is measured against when an RPC node saw the first shred, which lags the leader.
- A one-node local cluster cannot test this claim: its validator leads every slot, so nothing waits in a buffer.

## Operating characteristics

`python -m tiepairs power` simulates this whole rule with 90 planned pairs per group and 90% landing per probe. If treated
validators differ by σ = 0.05 and controls by σ = 0.15, the rule says "supported" in 65.3% of runs at 0.90 vs 0.58, 86.7% at
0.95 vs 0.58 and 93.9% at 0.98 vs 0.58. If treated validators differ as much as controls, σ = 0.15 in both, that falls to
40.2%, 56.1% and 64.2%. With no version effect and σ = 0.15 in both groups, the false-alarm rate is 0.1% at a shared rate
of 0.58, 1.3% at 0.71, 3.0% at 0.80 and 2.7% at 0.90.

Validators on one version can differ: in the baseline, 30/42 on Agave against 15/36 on Jito-Solana, two-sided p = 0.011.
That is why the rule asks for a minimum difference and a result on every treated validator.
